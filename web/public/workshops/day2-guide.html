Eric opens Rep, selects the training and existing agents across teams, confirms the timezone, and creates the session. The creator is the default presenter and follow-up coach. Keep the presenter console on your screen and share the separate presentation window from the same browser. Click an answer on your presenter slide to highlight it in both windows. Ask for the reasoning, then click Submit answer to share the explanation. Open Presenter notes in the toolbar for the current slide; those notes stay private. People without HQ can answer aloud and explain their choice; this discussion selection does not create an individual submission.

Ask each selected agent to open the session through their own existing login. No second invitation is needed. Confirm that everyone can join and see the current activity. Submit written work before showing the model. Give one correction, then ask for a targeted retry. Advance to the question. Agents answer and submit on that slide, and their answers appear automatically beside your slide. Review actual arrivals before revealing the example. If someone needs more time, pause or return to the slide.

In self-paced study, notes remain in the learner’s browser until they choose to share them. In a live session, the presenter sees submitted answers and progress; unsubmitted writing stays private. Preserve first attempts; a retry after an example is assisted.

For remote practice, use the meeting platform’s breakout rooms. Assign pairs or trios and rotate agent, buyer, and observer. In pairs, the buyer gives feedback. Every agent needs a speaking turn, one correction, and a retry. If a partner is absent, assign a coach or leave the observation outstanding.

Conversation starters to keep beside you

Choose what fits the conversation. You do not need to ask every question. Start with what the buyer already told you and follow their answers.

Open the conversation

  1. What made you click on this particular home?
  2. Have you had a chance to look at other homes in the market?
  3. What other homes have you been considering?
  4. What have you liked or disliked about the homes you’ve seen?
  5. Is this your first home purchase, or have you bought before?

Understand what matters

  1. Where are you moving from?
  2. Which areas are you considering, and what draws you to them?
  3. What is working well in your current home?
  4. What would you like your next home to do differently?
  5. What prompted you to start looking?
  6. What would that extra space let you do?

Explore timing and decisions

  1. What timing would work best for you?
  2. What needs to happen before you can make a move?
  3. Who else would you like involved in looking at homes or making the decision?

Follow an answer

  1. Tell me more about that.
  2. What makes that important to you?
  3. What would that change for you?
  4. What would help you feel comfortable with the next step?

When a buyer raises a concern

  1. Acknowledge the concern: recognize the worry or frustration before moving on.
  2. Ask questions to understand: What part concerns you most? What happened before? What would you need to know?
  3. Offer a solution: propose a specific action tied to their answer, then check whether it would help.

Useful next steps may include verifying property information, comparing homes, arranging lender help when the buyer raises financing, or agreeing on a later conversation. Be accurate about your role, property status, and what you can promise. Respect the buyer’s decision.

What each signal means

Connection status is separate from participation. A submitted answer is separate from a completed action. A record check is separate from writing quality. Record who observed a speaking turn and who reviewed it; a peer observation or self-check is not coach sign-off. Practice readiness, quiz certification, and activation remain separate.
