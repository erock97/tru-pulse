How Zillow Preferred works

Zillow introduces the customer. You build the relationship.

The program

Zillow Preferred is an invitation-only program, previously called Flex. Zillow connects you with potential buyers. A success fee applies when a transaction covered by your agreement closes. Review that agreement with your team.

Your role

Be available, understand the customer’s request, agree on a useful next step, and keep the record current. The customer may be new to buying and may not know who is calling.

Growth

Your performance and the areas you serve help determine how many leads you receive. Tell the team how many new leads you can handle each month. The limit is 15 per month, or 10 in your first month. You can ask for fewer. The maximum is not a guaranteed number of leads.

Official reference: Zillow Preferred

Before your lead funnel turns on

Complete the Zillow setup as well as the TrueHQ training.

Agreement & account

Use your Zillow onboarding invitation and your own account. Complete the Zillow Preferred agent agreement and required onboarding. Ask the team to verify activation; completing this class alone does not activate routing.

ZIP preferences

When you log in for onboarding, set the ZIP preferences available to you. Choose areas you can serve well. Confirm your final routed ZIPs with the team lead; preferences and team routing must agree.

Capacity & availability

Tell the team how many new leads you can handle alongside your existing clients. The maximum is 15 per month, or 10 in your first month as a new hire. Confirm your number and availability before leads begin.

Official reference: Program policies · Agent capacity & routing · Program benefits

The standards you must meet

Know the results we expect, the lead limits, and the tools you must use.

1 in 30

Closing standard

Our team standard is one closing for every 30 leads you take.

25%+

Pickup rate

Answer at least 25% of incoming Zillow connection calls over the last 90 days. Answering 10 of 40 incoming calls is 25%. This measures the calls you answer, not how often buyers answer you.

Monthly lead limits

You can receive up to 15 leads per month. New hires can receive up to 10 in their first month. These are maximums, not guaranteed lead counts.

Follow Up Boss is mandatory

Work your leads in Follow Up Boss. All lead calls and texts must go through Follow Up Boss so the communication is saved on the contact record.

Meet 100% of your assigned Zillow Home Loans pre-approval target over three months. Ask your team lead what your target is. We cover lender introductions and buyer choice on Day 4.

The closing standard and monthly caps above are TRU team rules. Zillow program standards

The welcome text has already gone out

Read what the customer received before you make your personal contact.

On arrival

TRU sends an automated initial text. Read the contact history to understand the inquiry and prior messages. An automated greeting does not mean you spoke with the buyer.

Your outreach

Use FUB’s calling or texting controls on the record so communication is captured. Introduce yourself, your brokerage and the Zillow request. Invite an appropriate next step; listen before assuming readiness.

After the call

Save the outcome and context, confirm the true stage/timeframe, and create the next task or agreed appointment. Make your calls and send your texts through FUB. If a customer reaches you elsewhere, record what happened and continue the follow-up through FUB.

TRU standard: at least five unique personal calls or texts in the first seven days from arrival. Automated messages and emails do not count; day seven is not a stop rule.

Demo: update the stage after a call

A stage tells the team what has happened with this customer.

Choose the right stage

An unanswered call is Attempted contact. A real conversation with no meeting booked is Spoke with customer. A meeting with an agreed time and place is Appointment set.

Where to change it

On the left side of the contact record, find Stage in the Details panel. Click the current stage, choose the new one, and save it. In our simulator, the green check saves the change.

When to update it

Update the stage after the call, while the outcome is fresh. If an automatic update appears, check that it matches the conversation.

Watch the next screen

Your presenter will show Avery’s record, select Appointment set, and save it. Avery has agreed to Saturday at 11 at the property. A tentative “Saturday might work” would not be enough.

Recognize Smart Actions after a call

Read the suggested action. Confirm it against the conversation.

After an eligible FUB call, Smart Actions may suggest a stage or appointment by text. Review the customer, action, and date before approving. Timeframe changes are automatic. No text does not mean there is no follow-up to do.

Jamie says Friday afternoon might work, but needs to check with a partner. No time or property is agreed. A Smart Actions message suggests an appointment for Friday at 3. You also notice the move timeframe changed.

You still owe Jamie a call tomorrow. Inspect the proposed action and the actual conversation. Decide what to accept, decline, or correct, and what work remains yours.

Look for the purple update marker and change log. Check Appointments for duplicates; appointments created by FUB identify that in the description. Keep using the manual record controls when needed.

What My Agent means

A Zillow Preferred connection starts a 180-day My Agent relationship.

Where to check

Open the contact → Details → My Agent status. This tells you whether the Zillow relationship is active, waiting for a response, or ending. The contact’s stage tells you where you are in the buying process.

Why it matters

While the relationship is active, Zillow shows you as the customer’s agent and can share their Zillow home-search activity with you. Check the expiration date. The customer can end this connection before the 180 days are up.

How to use it

Use what the customer is exploring to ask a better question and offer useful help. Maintain a real conversation. My Agent does not replace the required buyer or touring agreement.

Official reference: My Agent · Touring agreements

See what the buyer is viewing on Zillow

On the right side of the contact record, open Zillow Insights.

A change worth asking about

Last Tuesday, Taylor said a three-bedroom home near work mattered most, but had not settled on a budget. Today, eligible Zillow activity shows saved homes in another ZIP and a higher price range.

You have no new reply from Taylor. A partner might be browsing, or the criteria might have changed. You do not know yet.

Look at the date range and the actual saved/viewed homes. Offer a useful comparison and ask whether the search has changed. Treat the price range as browsing context, not approved purchasing power.

Official reference: Zillow Insights

A useful message

“Taylor, last time we spoke you wanted a three-bedroom near work. Are those still your priorities, or would comparing another area be helpful?” Ask about the search without assuming that saved homes establish a budget.

Demo: save a useful contact note

The note should let you or a teammate continue the conversation without starting over.

Read the history first

The center of the record shows previous notes, calls, and texts. Before contacting someone, read the latest entries so you know what has already happened.

What to write

Record the buyer’s request, useful preferences, what you agreed, and what still needs an answer. For Avery: buying with their sister, Olympia or Lacey, three or more bedrooms, and hoping to move before November. You promised two options by Thursday.

Where to save it

Type in the note box at the top of the contact history, then click Create Note. The note should appear in the history below. Text left in the box has not been saved.

Watch, then practice

On the next screen, your presenter will save a note and reopen the history to show it. You will then do the same. A note records the promise; a task reminds you to carry it out.

Write a note another agent can use

Fictional example: Avery agreed to a Saturday visit, but access still needs confirmation.

“Avery wants an enclosed office for daily work calls. Saturday at 11 works for the buyer. Property access is pending. I will confirm access and call Avery by 5 today. If the time is unavailable, we will agree on another option.”

The note separates the buyer’s preference from confirmed access. Create the follow-up task separately so the promise appears in your work queue.

Demo: schedule the next call

A task tells you what to do and when to do it.

Task or appointment?

Use a task for work you owe, such as calling a buyer or checking parking. Use an appointment for a meeting the customer has agreed to attend at a specific time.

Open Tasks

On the right side of the record, find Tasks and click +. Name the action clearly, choose the responsible agent, and set a due date and time. Save the task.

Make it useful

“Call Avery tomorrow at 9:30 about the Juniper inquiry” is useful. “Follow up” leaves you guessing. If someone else takes over, make sure they have access to the contact and know what to do.

Watch, then practice

Your presenter will create the task on the next screen and show its date in the Tasks panel. Then you will record an unanswered call and schedule another attempt. An unanswered call does not tell you why the buyer missed it.

Which tool holds which part of the work?

Here is how the tools work together when a teammate covers a showing.

A covering agent needs a usable handoff

Avery agrees to tour 406 Juniper on Saturday at 11. You promised to check parking with the listing agent tomorrow. A teammate will cover the showing and needs access to Avery’s background. Avery’s sister is a co-buyer, not a member of your team. You also received a relevant approved document.

You need to preserve the buyer’s preferences, record the meeting, assign your parking follow-up, and prepare the covering agent. No offer has been accepted.

Official reference: Contact record · Tasks

Note and appointment

Save the buyer’s preferences in a contact note. Put the confirmed Saturday 11 showing in Appointments.

Task and collaborator

Make a dated task for checking parking. Add the covering teammate as a collaborator if they need contact access, and tell them what they are responsible for.

Files and relationships

Store the approved document in Files. Record the sister as a related contact or co-buyer. She is not a team collaborator. Add a Deal when the transaction reaches the team’s agreed point.

Repair Avery’s record

You are covering the next call. Decide which parts of this record you can rely on.

A hopeful plan became an appointment label

Yesterday Avery called about 406 and 422 Juniper Lane. Avery said, “Let’s plan on Thursday morning — I’ll confirm once I’ve checked with my sister.” No appointment was booked. You are now covering for the agent who took the call.

The record says Appointment set. The saved note says, “Talked. Interested. Follow up later.” There is no next task. Avery’s interest in the two properties is visible, but the note does not tell the next caller which homes were discussed or what confirmation is still needed.

Before you edit, decide what the record claims, what the buyer actually confirmed, and what another agent needs next. In the following exercise, identify the record’s problems first, then make your changes. Do not create a meeting just to make the label look right.

Before anyone edits, your presenter will point out the stage, the vague note, and the missing task. On the next screen, identify those problems, then use the same stage, note, and task controls you have already practiced.

The handoff test

You will be unavailable when the next task is due. Your teammate has only this record. Could they identify the buyer’s request, what was actually agreed, and the action they own? If not, name the missing fact rather than adding a more optimistic stage.

Handle a reply from start to finish

Read the conversation in Inbox, open the contact record, and complete or schedule the follow-up.

Read the whole thread

Check My Inbox and the team inboxes assigned to you. Match the person before replying. Unread is a viewing state; opening a message does not complete the customer’s request.

Preserve the outcome

Save important customer context on the contact record. An internal Inbox note stays in the conversation and is not added to the lead profile. A draft has not been sent.

Finish or hand off

Reply, handle the request or assign follow-up with an owner and due date. Close the conversation after handling it. Read status or closing an Inbox item does not update the contact stage.

Official reference: Inbox

Use Smart Lists and tasks together

Check replies, due tasks, and today’s appointments. Then work through your contact lists.

Inbox + due work

Handle incoming replies, missed calls and due/overdue commitments. Confirm today’s appointments. A specific promise needs a dated task and an owner.

People → Smart Lists

Saved filters identify contacts by criteria such as stage or last communication. They update as records change. Use the team’s assigned lists; inspect filters if someone is missing.

Read before contacting

Confirm identity, source, owner, last conversation, recent activity and next task. Being absent from a list does not prove follow-up is done; access and filters affect what you see.

A practical start to your workday

Start with the work that already has a deadline.

Check what needs an answer

Open Inbox for new replies and missed calls. Check today’s appointments so you know when you need to be available.

Keep existing promises

Open Tasks and look for overdue work and calls due soon. If you cannot make a promised call, arrange coverage and leave a clear note.

Work new leads

Read the new inquiry and any automatic messages. Call through FUB, save the outcome, and schedule the next step. Use your team’s Smart Lists to keep working your contacts.

Define nurture: two different decisions

Maya Chen · The move is later, but there is a promise due

How it started: Maya requested a tour through Zillow three weeks ago. You spoke and toured one home together. She is renting until March and said, “I probably won’t move before my lease ends, but I want to understand the neighborhoods before I choose.”

What happened this week: On Tuesday she sent two listings and asked which area would make the commute easier. You agreed to call Friday at 4 p.m. She replied, “Yes, talk then.” She has not asked to stop hearing from you, and no second showing is booked.

What you inherit: The stage is Met with customer. A Friday 4 p.m. call task is assigned to you. A covering agent added, “Six months out — move to Nurture?” You have not discussed how often Maya wants contact or whether she would consider a suitable home sooner.

The decision: It is Friday at 3:45 p.m. Would you change the stage now, keep it while you clarify something, or take another step? What would you ask on the agreed call? Explain how your plan respects her timing and the promise already made.

Jordan Ellis · The record is quiet, but look at the attempts

How it started: Jordan requested information about 18 Cedar Street through Zillow on Monday morning. You called that morning, reached voicemail, and left a message offering to arrange a showing. You have never spoken with Jordan.

What happened this week: An automatic welcome text went out Monday. On Wednesday you personally texted, “I can help arrange a visit to Cedar Street — would Thursday afternoon or Saturday morning work?” There has been no reply. The record shows two property views on Thursday; it does not explain why Jordan viewed them.

What you inherit: It is Friday. The stage is Attempted contact. There is no next task. Another agent wrote, “No response — maybe not serious. Nurture?” The history contains one personal call and one personal text; the automatic message is separate.

The decision: What can you fairly conclude about Jordan’s interest? What would you do before treating this as a longer-term relationship? Choose a next attempt that offers a reason to respond, and explain when you would review the result. You do not know whether Jordan is busy, already helped, or no longer looking.

For Maya and Jordan, separate what you know from what you are assuming. Explain the next action you would take and why.

For each buyer, what do you know from the record, what is still unknown, and would you change the stage yet? Explain your reasoning.

What would you do next for each buyer? Include what you would say or ask, who owns the action, and when you would do it.

What new information would make you change your plan? Name a specific answer or event, and when you would review the record.

What each signal means

Connection status is separate from participation. A submitted answer is separate from a completed action. A record check is separate from writing quality.
