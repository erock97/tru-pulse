The opening answers “Who is this?”

Jordan requested Saturday morning. Use that information.

Agent “Hi Jordan, I’m Sam with Northside Realty, a featured partner with Zillow. I’m calling about your request to see 1234 Lane.”

Agent “I saw Saturday morning. I’ll check access for that time. Would morning still work for you?”

Jordan “Yes, morning works.”

No time selected? Offer two times you can actually make. Property access still needs confirmation.

The appointment opens a conversation

Before asking about the search, explain why the questions help.

Agent “Do you have a few minutes so I can learn what you’re looking for and make our time together useful?”

Jordan “Sure. We’re already in town.”

Agent “What’s changing where you live now?”

Jordan “We rent an apartment. We’d like outdoor space.”

If they have only a minute, keep it brief. Handle the request and agreed next action without forcing a full interview.

“Outdoor space” needs a follow-up

The feature becomes useful when you understand its purpose.

Agent “What would you like to do with that space?”

Jordan “I’d like a vegetable garden.”

Agent “What would you need from the space for that?”

Jordan “Somewhere sunny, with room for raised beds.”

Now the search has a reason: usable garden space. A large yard alone may not solve it.

What if the buyer only wants an answer?

Variation: the buyer declines a tour and asks about the roof.

Agent “Of course. What would you like to know?”

Buyer “How old is the roof?”

Agent “I’ll check that. Can I ask what concerns you about it?”

Buyer “I’m worried about taking on repairs.”

Bring the answer back. Explain how a viewing could help them decide. If they still decline, agree how to return the information.

When the buyer asks about a lender

Variation: “Should I talk to a lender before we look?”

Agent “A lender can help you understand your options. What are you hoping to work out before we look?”

Buyer “I don’t know what payment I’d be comfortable with.”

Agent “That’s useful to work through. I can help connect you with a lender to discuss those numbers.”

Help with the concern. Do not turn the showing request into a demand for preapproval.

“Are you the listing agent?”

The buyer needs a clear answer about your role.

If they prefer the listing agent, offer to help with that connection. Respect their choice.

The home is under contract

Separate variation: you learn this during the first inquiry. No showing is confirmed.

Agent “The home is under contract. I can check whether a viewing is still possible. What drew you to it?”

Buyer “The yard. I want room for a garden.”

Agent “I can also research homes with useful garden space. Would that help?”

State what you know. Keep access and unresearched alternatives conditional.

The plan should use what Jordan told you

Back to the original call. Jordan wants sunny space for raised beds.

Agent “Would it help to compare another home with usable garden space while we’re out?”

Jordan “Yes, that would help.”

Agent “Saturday morning is your preference. I’ll check access to 1234 Lane and research a comparison with garden space. I’ll text you the details after I hear back. Is that right?”

Jordan “Yes. Text is good.”

The whole call: opening and discovery

The same call: discovery and agreement

Continue without restarting the conversation.

Sam “What would you like to do with that space?”

Jordan “A vegetable garden. I need sun and room for raised beds.”

Sam “Would comparing another home with garden space help?”

Jordan “Yes.”

Sam “Saturday morning is your preference. I’ll check access and research that comparison, then text the details. Does that work?”

What each signal means

Connection status is separate from participation. A submitted answer is separate from a completed action. A record check is separate from writing quality. Record who observed a speaking turn and who reviewed it; a peer observation or self-check is not coach sign-off. Practice readiness, quiz certification, and activation remain separate.

After the workshop

Live session: closing the session creates assignments for recall within 24 hours, a fresh case in three days, and coach review in seven days. The session creator owns follow-up across the selected teams unless a different authorized coach was selected. Confirm the displayed session timezone. Due and overdue work appears in Rep; the daily digest links to work due or awaiting review.

Self-paced study: arrange those same checks with a coach. If there has been no relevant client opportunity, use a new fictional case and mark real-work application as not yet observed.

Keep the first attempt and correction. A useful follow-up asks whether the agent can apply the skill in another case.
