Three lead routes

Read the lead’s type and actual request before choosing your response.

Lead typeBuyer requestHow it reaches you
Connection / contact agentContact about a home for saleLive phone connection or FUB alert with contact preference
Real-time touringA tour, using availability supplied through ShowingTimeFUB alert; not a live call in TRU’s documented flow
Standard property tourA tour at a requested timeLive connection or FUB alert; requested time may still need availability checks

Example: “I requested 4 p.m.” on a standard tour is a requested time. “I’ll check access and confirm with you” keeps the request moving without promising unverified access.

Choose the response that fits

Calls help you hear tone, ask follow-up questions, and build a relationship.

Already connected

Introduce yourself and help with the request. You are already on the call.

New alert, no preference

Call within five minutes of receipt during TRU’s 8 a.m.–8 p.m. operating window. If unanswered, follow with a personal text.

Text first / requested callback

Acknowledge by text now; honor the requested call time or explicit no-call preference.

If the first call is unanswered

Make it easy for the buyer to recognize you and respond.

  1. Call promptly

    Place the call within five minutes of receipt in the normal flow. Leave an opening voicemail when appropriate.

  2. Follow with a personal text

    Connect to the real inquiry and invite a phone conversation with genuine availability.

  3. Keep ownership

    Continue useful outreach while unanswered. The automated introduction is separate from your work.

“Text now” and “call at five”

Acknowledge the buyer now while respecting the plan they requested.

“Text is better right now.”

“Of course. I can help here. A quick call would also let us talk through the home and your plans—would this evening work?” Text now; do not record a permanent no-call preference.

“Call me at five.”

“Hi Maya, I’m Sam with Northside Realty, the agent who’ll call at five about your request. Let me know if you have questions before then.” Keep five unless the buyer agrees to change it.

“Please do not call me.”

Honor that request. Give useful help through the agreed channel.

ALMS is your call framework

Start with a clear introduction. Then help with the appointment before discovery.

Location and motivation can flow in either order. Listen to the person; do not recite a checklist.

Take the first turn

Orient the buyer: your name, brokerage, Zillow connection, and reason for the conversation.

Live connection

“Zillow connected us about your request…” fits a buyer already on the line. Begin warmly instead of waiting in silence.

Outbound call

“I’m calling about your request…” connects an unfamiliar number to something the buyer recognizes.

Use your real brokerage and role. Do not borrow claims about expertise or relationships that are not yours.

Ask permission, then learn what fits

Explain why the questions will help the buyer.

Location

What attracted you to this area? What makes that location work for you?

Follow the answer

If they mention a shorter commute, ask what would improve. Do not jump to the next checklist item.

M · Motivation

Move from the feature to the reason behind it.

Buyer “We really want a bigger yard.”

Agent “What would you like to be able to do with that space?”

Buyer “I want room for a vegetable garden.”

Agent “That helps. What would the garden need from the space?”

“Big yard” is a feature. Gardening gives it meaning. Do not assume dogs, children, entertaining, or any other reason.

S · Summarize

Give the buyer back their priorities and the agreed next step.

Name what is agreed, what still needs checking, and who will do it. Invite the buyer to correct you.

“I just have questions.”

Help with the question; earn another invitation through useful service.

  1. Acknowledge and capture

    “Of course—what would you like to know?” Answer accurately; say what you need to check.

  2. Learn the context

    With permission, understand what drew them to the home and how the answer affects their search.

  3. Offer a useful next step

    Explain how seeing the home could help. If they still decline, stop pushing and agree how you’ll bring back the answers.

Answers are not a reward for agreeing to a showing. Never guess or hide a known answer.

Keep the first meeting easy

Do not open with a demand for preapproval, budget, or proof they are ready to buy.

Why timing matters

They asked to see a home. A private financial interview from someone they do not know can turn that request into a barrier.

If the buyer asks

Answer their question. Learn enough context to make guidance useful; involve a lender for financing specifics.

The usual later opening

The end of the first showing offers context for a relevant financing conversation. A long buying timeline does not automatically rule out planning help.

Answer honestly and keep helping

Answer the role question clearly, then return to how you can help.

Your role

“I’m not the listing agent. I help buyers and can coordinate with the listing side. What would you like to know?”

Availability

If the home is under contract, say so. Check any uncertain access; do not promise a showing.

Useful next step

Ask what attracted them to the home and offer relevant options, with their permission.

Permission “Have you got a few minutes so I can make our outing useful?”

Jordan: “It would shorten my commute.”

…to a clear next step

The next question comes from what the buyer says.

Motivation “What else caught your eye?”
Jordan: “The yard. I’d like a vegetable garden.”
“What would you need from that space?”

Plan “Would you like to compare a couple of other homes while we’re out?”

Summarize “The commute and garden space matter. Saturday morning is your preference. I’ll check access, research the comparisons, and send the details. Is that right?”

Then briefly explain the applicable touring form and the follow-up you are actually committing to.

Follow through on what you promised

The next contact should have a reason the buyer recognizes.

After a conversation

Record their priorities, contact preference, pending access checks, and the exact next action you promised.

If unanswered

Day one: aim for three to four personal touches, at least two calls and one text. First week: ten total including day one, mostly calls.

Once they reply

Follow the conversation and agreed plan. Stop applying the unanswered-lead quota.

Set a task for the promised action. Use the buyer’s agreed channel and timing; a reply ends the unanswered-lead quota, not your responsibility to follow through.

What each signal means

Connection status is separate from participation. A submitted answer is separate from a completed action. A record check is separate from writing quality. Record who observed a speaking turn and who reviewed it; a peer observation or self-check is not coach sign-off. Practice readiness, quiz certification, and activation remain separate.
