Show like a pro

73 minutes including practice · 19 screens

Agent worksheetOpen presentation

Before you begin

Keep this guide on a second screen or print it. Project the presentation window. Arrange pairs or trios and have paper available. Ask everyone to answer before revealing an example. After feedback, ask the agent to retry the specific part that needs work.

The timer supports short practice rounds. For longer rounds, use your room clock. Practice notes save on each device; they are not sent to the facilitator. Use hand votes or partner discussion for group responses.

What to listen for

The agent learns specific preferences and timing, handles financing preparation in context, and agrees on a useful next action.

Facilitator notes

01 / 19 · Start · 2 min

Show like a pro

Invite a recent example from one person, then begin. Keep the opening to two minutes.

Ask the room: Ask: What would you like to get better at today?

02 / 19 · Start · 3 min

What makes a showing uncomfortable?

Take three short answers. Ask agents to describe behavior rather than personality. Connect those answers to space, useful questions, and an agreed next step.

Ask the room: What could you stop doing on your next showing?

03 / 19 · Prepare · 3 min

Confirm access and the required agreement

Use the form and instructions approved by your brokerage. Zillow’s current product page confirms its seven-day touring agreement. That term describes this product, not every buyer agreement. Confirm the current product and local requirements before teaching the form. This is a process check, not legal advice.

Ask the room: How would you explain the agreement before the appointment?

04 / 19 · Prepare · 3 min

Give the buyer useful comparisons

Use actual availability when preparing a real tour. Avoid adding homes just to make the tour longer. Ask agents to explain one useful comparison.

Ask the room: What would make a second home worth including?

05 / 19 · At the home · 3 min

Let the buyer look first

Ask someone to demonstrate an opening at the door. Respect seller instructions and actual safety needs. Availability matters more than standing in one prescribed place.

Ask the room: What will you say before the buyer starts looking?

06 / 19 · At the home · 3 min

Let the buyer have their own preferences

Use a fictional vaulted-ceiling example from the source material. Distinguish personal taste from material or safety concerns that must be addressed accurately.

Ask the room: When does an observation need to be raised even if it is not your preference?

07 / 19 · After the showing · 3 min

Ask permission to compare impressions

Have the buyer role say no first. The agent should accept it comfortably and ask a useful follow-up. A negative answer is useful information.

Ask the room: What would you ask after “None of them felt right”?

08 / 19 · After the showing · 3 min

Use a rating to start a conversation

Ask partners to give the same rating for different reasons. Show why the number alone is not enough for a note or a search decision.

Ask the room: How would two buyers’ eights mean different things?

09 / 19 · After the showing · 3 min

Ask about financing in context

This is a conversation after the showing, distinct from the first-call LEAD sequence. Do not collect private income or credit details in a sidewalk conversation.

Ask the room: What would you ask if they say their bank has already helped?

10 / 19 · After the showing · 3 min

Ask what is behind the timing

Ask permission before exploring a sensitive reason. Do not assume a round number is a firm deadline. If they mention a lease, ask about the actual date and flexibility.

Ask the room: What changes if the lease ends on a fixed date?

11 / 19 · Plan the next step · 3 min

Match the plan to this buyer

Ask agents to explain why the same next step would not fit all three buyers. Use actual market, lender, and transaction timelines; do not teach a fixed number of days as universal.

Ask the room: Which next step would be premature for this buyer?

12 / 19 · Plan the next step · 4 min

Work backward from a real deadline

Use a fictional lease-end example. Avoid presenting an estimated closing date as a guarantee. Have the buyer repeat the plan to catch different expectations.

Ask the room: What needs confirmation before you promise a date?

13 / 19 · Practice · 4 min

Practice the first question after a showing

Pairs take 60 seconds each with 30 seconds feedback and a short retry. Do not stack all five questions into one long sentence.

Ask the room: Did the buyer have enough time to answer?

14 / 19 · Practice · 4 min

Practice a conversation about timing

Give each partner a different response. Ask the agent to learn whether the date is fixed, flexible, or still uncertain. Do not pressure the buyer to move earlier.

Ask the room: What did you learn that changes the plan?

15 / 19 · Practice · 16 min

Run the conversation after a showing

Three rounds: 3-minute conversation, 1-minute feedback, 1-minute retry. Rotate all roles. Judge the usefulness of the questions and plan, not whether the agent recites every example word for word.

Ask the room: Observer: what did the agent learn that would change the next step?

16 / 19 · Update the record · 3 min

Record what the showing changed

Correct the source deck’s blanket instruction to use Showing homes after every showing. Ask an agent to explain the stage based on the actual event.

Ask the room: Would another agent know why you chose the next homes?

17 / 19 · Retain · 4 min

Write the follow-up record

This is a coach-reviewed written exercise, not an automated grade. Partners compare against the criteria and revise one missing detail.

Ask the room: Which detail could someone act on tomorrow?

18 / 19 · Retain · 3 min

What would you ask without the slides?

Give 45 seconds without notes. Accept natural wording and a conversation that follows the buyer. Correct an omitted topic through a short spoken retry.

Ask the room: Which answer most often changes your plan?

19 / 19 · Retain · 3 min

Before your next client conversation

Ask each agent to name one behavior they will practice. Within 24 hours, ask them to explain it without notes. In three days, use a new scenario. In seven days, review an actual example. These are suggested coaching intervals; no reminders are sent.

Ask the room: Tell your partner what you will practice and when.

Buyer practice cards

Jordan · ready to offer

“The second house is a nine. I would like to offer, but I have not sent my lender the documents.”

Learn why it is a nine. Clarify preparation and agree on a useful next step.

Morgan · fixed deadline

“The layout was good, but neither had the outdoor space I need. My lease ends November 30.”

Learn the missing criteria and whether the date has flexibility. Agree on the next search and appointment.

Alex · flexible timing

“Nothing felt quite right. I said six months, but I am not working toward a deadline.”

Accept the answer. Learn what was missing and agree on a follow-up the buyer finds useful.

Practice after the workshop

Within 24 hours, explain the lesson without notes and practice with a partner. In three days, try another buyer case. In seven days, review an actual example with your coach. These are suggested intervals, not scheduled reminders.
