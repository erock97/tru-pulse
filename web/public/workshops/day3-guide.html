Before you begin

Keep the private presenter console on your screen and share the separate presentation window. Confirm the roster, team, signed-in account, invitation, training availability, and named coach. A join link identifies the session; each agent still needs authorization.

Ask each person to join through their own login. Submit written work before showing the model. Give one correction, then ask for a targeted retry. Open activities under presenter control; advancing leaves unfinished work available for catch-up. Show help requests and connection problems separately.

In self-paced study, notes remain in the learner’s browser until they choose to share them. In a live session, the presenter sees submitted answers and progress; unsubmitted writing stays private. Preserve first attempts; a retry after an example is assisted.

For remote practice, use the meeting platform’s breakout rooms. Assign pairs or trios and rotate agent, buyer, and observer. In pairs, the buyer gives feedback. Every agent needs a speaking turn, one correction, and a retry. If a partner is absent, assign a coach or leave the observation outstanding.

What each signal means

Connection status is separate from participation. A submitted answer is separate from a completed action. A record check is separate from writing quality. Record who observed a speaking turn and who reviewed it; a peer observation or self-check is not coach sign-off. Practice readiness, quiz certification, and activation remain separate.

Facilitator flow

01 / 19 · 0–3 min · Start with practice

“None of them felt right.”

Have every agent submit an individual response before revealing the model. Compare the main pattern in the room, then demonstrate once. The choice is a warm-up, not proof of skill; the next activity is an observed attempt.

Ask the room: Submit your choice and wording before the example.

Participant task: None of them felt right. What do you say next?

  • Defend the second home.
  • Ask what was missing.
  • Book another tour without changing the search.
  • Write your next question.
  • After hearing the buyer’s answer, record what changes in your plan.

After submission: Ask what was missing. Accept the buyer’s reaction and learn a specific preference. That answer should change the next search or conversation; a rating by itself does not explain what matters.

Reveal after the first response:

That’s useful to know. What was missing for you? [Listen.] When you say the workspace felt separate, what would work better? [Listen.] Let’s use that to adjust what we look at next.

02 / 19 · 3–8 min · Practice

Practice the first question after a showing

Model one brief exchange after the first submission. Give every agent a speaking turn, one specific correction, and a targeted retry. Use the meeting platform for breakout rooms. In pairs, the buyer supplies the observation; in trios, rotate agent, buyer, observer. Do not label a peer or self-check as coach sign-off. If no partner is present, assign a coach or leave the observation outstanding. Give the buyer room to explain a preference. A completed list of questions is not the objective.

Ask the room: Did the buyer have enough time to answer?

Participant task: Let the buyer answer before asking the next question.

  • What did the buyer say that changed your plan?
  • What did you improve after feedback?

Observer criteria:

  • Accepts the buyer’s reaction
  • Asks a relevant follow-up
  • Learns a specific preference
  • Uses the answer to change the plan

03 / 19 · 8–11 min · Start

What makes a showing uncomfortable?

Take three short answers. Ask agents to describe behavior rather than personality. Connect those answers to space, useful questions, and an agreed next step.

Ask the room: What could you stop doing on your next showing?

Participant task: Start with the buyer’s experience.

  • What would you do differently?

04 / 19 · 11–14 min · Prepare

Confirm access and the required agreement

Use the form and instructions approved by your brokerage. Zillow’s current product page confirms its seven-day touring agreement. That term describes this product, not every buyer agreement. Confirm the current product and local requirements before teaching the form. This is a process check, not legal advice.

Ask the room: How would you explain the agreement before the appointment?

05 / 19 · 14–17 min · Prepare

Give the buyer useful comparisons

Use actual availability when preparing a real tour. Avoid adding homes just to make the tour longer. Ask agents to explain one useful comparison.

Ask the room: What would make a second home worth including?

Participant task: Choose homes for a reason.

  • What would each home help this buyer compare?

06 / 19 · 17–20 min · At the home

Let the buyer look first

Ask someone to demonstrate an opening at the door. Respect seller instructions and actual safety needs. Availability matters more than standing in one prescribed place.

Ask the room: What will you say before the buyer starts looking?

07 / 19 · 20–23 min · At the home

Let the buyer have their own preferences

Use a fictional vaulted-ceiling example from the source material. Distinguish personal taste from material or safety concerns that must be addressed accurately.

Ask the room: When does an observation need to be raised even if it is not your preference?

Participant task: Your taste may be different from theirs.

  • Explain why your taste is better.
  • Ask what they like about it and listen.
  • Assume it makes the home wrong for them.

After submission: Ask what they like about it and listen. Yes. Learn the reason without projecting your preference.

08 / 19 · 23–26 min · After the showing

Ask permission to compare impressions

Have the buyer role say no first. The agent should accept it comfortably and ask a useful follow-up. A negative answer is useful information.

Ask the room: What would you ask after “None of them felt right”?

09 / 19 · 26–29 min · After the showing

Use a rating to start a conversation

Ask partners to give the same rating for different reasons. Show why the number alone is not enough for a note or a search decision.

Ask the room: How would two buyers’ eights mean different things?

Participant task: The reason matters more than the number.

  • What did you learn that should change the next search?

10 / 19 · 29–32 min · After the showing

Ask about financing in context

This is a conversation after the showing, distinct from the first-call LEAD sequence. Do not collect private income or credit details in a sidewalk conversation.

Ask the room: What would you ask if they say their bank has already helped?

11 / 19 · 32–35 min · After the showing

Ask what is behind the timing

Ask permission before exploring a sensitive reason. Do not assume a round number is a firm deadline. If they mention a lease, ask about the actual date and flexibility.

Ask the room: What changes if the lease ends on a fixed date?

Participant task: A date is more useful when you understand why it matters.

  • Write a question that invites the buyer to explain their timing.

12 / 19 · 35–38 min · Plan the next step

Match the plan to this buyer

Ask agents to explain why the same next step would not fit all three buyers. Use actual market, lender, and transaction timelines; do not teach a fixed number of days as universal.

Ask the room: Which next step would be premature for this buyer?

13 / 19 · 38–42 min · Plan the next step

Work backward from a real deadline

Use a fictional lease-end example. Avoid presenting an estimated closing date as a guarantee. Have the buyer repeat the plan to catch different expectations.

Ask the room: What needs confirmation before you promise a date?

Participant task: Confirm the steps with the people responsible for them.

  • Write the next appointment and what it is for.

14 / 19 · 42–46 min · Practice

Practice a conversation about timing

Give each partner a different response. Ask the agent to learn whether the date is fixed, flexible, or still uncertain. Do not pressure the buyer to move earlier.

Ask the room: What did you learn that changes the plan?

Participant task: Ask what makes the date important.

  • Choose a response. Write your next question.

15 / 19 · 46–62 min · Practice

Run the conversation after a showing

Three rounds: 3-minute conversation, 1-minute feedback, 1-minute retry. Rotate all roles. Judge the usefulness of the questions and plan, not whether the agent recites every example word for word. Give every agent a speaking turn, one specific correction, and a targeted retry. Use the meeting platform for breakout rooms. In pairs, the buyer supplies the observation; in trios, rotate agent, buyer, observer. Do not label a peer or self-check as coach sign-off. If no partner is present, assign a coach or leave the observation outstanding.

Ask the room: Observer: what did the agent learn that would change the next step?

Participant task: Agent, buyer, observer. Rotate after each round.

  • What specific correction will you retry?
  • What changed in your retry?

Observer criteria:

  • Accepts the buyer’s reaction
  • Asks a relevant follow-up
  • Learns a specific preference
  • Uses the answer to change the plan
  • Clarifies financing preparation and the reason for timing in context
  • Agrees on a useful next action, date, and owner

16 / 19 · 62–65 min · Update the record

Record what the showing changed

Correct the source deck’s blanket instruction to use Showing homes after every showing. Ask an agent to explain the stage based on the actual event.

Ask the room: Would another agent know why you chose the next homes?

17 / 19 · 65–69 min · Retain

Write the follow-up record

This is a coach-reviewed written exercise, not an automated grade. Partners compare against the criteria and revise one missing detail. Submit the first written record before feedback. Then revise one specific part and submit a new attempt so the original remains visible.

Ask the room: Which detail could someone act on tomorrow?

Participant task: Use the practice buyer you just worked with.

  • Write the true stage, a factual note, and a dated task.

Reveal after the first response:

Stage matches the event. Note includes specific preferences and timing without guesses. Task names the agreed action and date. Ask your coach to review the record.

18 / 19 · 69–72 min · Retain

What would you ask without the slides?

Give 45 seconds without notes. Accept natural wording and a conversation that follows the buyer. Correct an omitted topic through a short spoken retry.

Ask the room: Which answer most often changes your plan?

Participant task: Recall the questions before checking the guide.

  • List the questions you would use after a showing.

Reveal after the first response:

Is any home worth pursuing? How close is it to what you want? What explains that rating? How will you prepare to purchase? What is behind your timing? Then summarize and agree on the next step.

19 / 19 · 72–75 min · Retain

Before your next client conversation

In live sessions, closing the session creates 24-hour recall, three-day fresh-case, and seven-day coach-review assignments. Confirm each agent’s named coach and session timezone. Agents see due work in Rep; the daily digest links to due or reviewable work. In self-paced study, arrange these checks with a coach. Without a relevant client opportunity, use a fresh fictional case and label real-work application not yet observed. Practice readiness is separate from quiz certification and activation.

Ask the room: Tell your partner what you will practice and when.

Participant task: Write down when you will practice again.

  • My practice partner, time, and focus

Buyer practice cards

Jordan · ready to offer

“The second house is a nine. I would like to offer, but I have not sent my lender the documents.”

Learn why it is a nine. Clarify preparation and agree on a useful next step.

Morgan · fixed deadline

“The layout was good, but neither had the outdoor space I need. My lease ends November 30.”

Learn the missing criteria and whether the date has flexibility. Agree on the next search and appointment.

Alex · flexible timing

“Nothing felt quite right. I said six months, but I am not working toward a deadline.”

Accept the answer. Learn what was missing and agree on a follow-up the buyer finds useful.

After the workshop

Live session: closing the session creates assignments for recall within 24 hours, a fresh case in three days, and coach review in seven days. Confirm the named coach and displayed session timezone. Due and overdue work appears in Rep; the daily digest links to work due or awaiting review.

Self-paced study: arrange those same checks with a coach. If there has been no relevant client opportunity, use a new fictional case and mark real-work application as not yet observed.

Keep the first attempt and correction. A useful follow-up asks whether the agent can apply the skill in another case.
