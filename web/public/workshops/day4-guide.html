Before you begin

Keep the private presenter console on your screen and share the separate presentation window. Confirm the roster, team, signed-in account, invitation, training availability, and named coach. A join link identifies the session; each agent still needs authorization.

Ask each person to join through their own login. Submit written work before showing the model. Give one correction, then ask for a targeted retry. Open activities under presenter control; advancing leaves unfinished work available for catch-up. Show help requests and connection problems separately.

In self-paced study, notes remain in the learner’s browser until they choose to share them. In a live session, the presenter sees submitted answers and progress; unsubmitted writing stays private. Preserve first attempts; a retry after an example is assisted.

For remote practice, use the meeting platform’s breakout rooms. Assign pairs or trios and rotate agent, buyer, and observer. In pairs, the buyer gives feedback. Every agent needs a speaking turn, one correction, and a retry. If a partner is absent, assign a coach or leave the observation outstanding.

What each signal means

Connection status is separate from participation. A submitted answer is separate from a completed action. A record check is separate from writing quality. Record who observed a speaking turn and who reviewed it; a peer observation or self-check is not coach sign-off. Practice readiness, quiz certification, and activation remain separate.

Facilitator flow

01 / 16 · 0–3 min · Start with practice

Jordan wants to make an offer

Have every agent submit an individual response before revealing the model. Compare the main pattern in the room, then demonstrate once. The choice is a warm-up, not proof of skill; the next activity is an observed attempt.

Ask the room: Submit your choice and wording before the example.

Participant task: Jordan wants to offer but has not sent documents to the bank. What next?

  • Promise approval today.
  • Clarify what the bank confirmed and offer help.
  • List every product benefit.
  • Write your introduction.
  • Write your permission question.
  • What is the next action if the buyer accepts?

After submission: Clarify what the bank confirmed and offer help. Clarify the buyer’s actual need before offering a relevant option. Explain one supported benefit, preserve lender choice, and confirm the next action only after consent. An introduction is optional; approval and savings cannot be promised.

Reveal after the first response:

Jordan, you want to be ready to offer, and we still need to confirm the financing. I work with a loan officer at Zillow Home Loans who can help you understand your options and next steps. Would an introduction be useful? You’re free to choose the lender that works best for you. [If accepted:] Is a call this afternoon a useful next step?

Practice your introduction

After the initial individual response, demonstrate the model in under one minute. Let every agent practice before naming Reflect, Explain, Ask, Confirm on the next screen. Give every agent a speaking turn, one specific correction, and a targeted retry. Use the meeting platform for breakout rooms. In pairs, the buyer supplies the observation; in trios, rotate agent, buyer, observer. Do not label a peer or self-check as coach sign-off. If no partner is present, assign a coach or leave the observation outstanding. Scheduling follows consent. A declined introduction is not a failed conversation.

Ask the room: Practice out loud. Reading silently does not count.

Participant task: Use Jordan’s situation. Keep it under a minute.

  • What one correction will you retry?
  • What changed in your retry?

Observer criteria:

  • Reflects the buyer’s actual concern
  • Explains one relevant supported benefit
  • Asks genuine permission and preserves buyer choice
  • Names an appropriate next action and owner; confirms after consent

03 / 16 · 9–12 min · Connect

How to introduce your loan officer

Name Reflect, Explain, Ask, Confirm using the practice that just happened. Ask which step helped the buyer decide. Explain that buyer choice is part of a useful conversation, not an objection-handling trick. Avoid hiding the lender’s identity behind a first name.

Ask the room: Where did your buyer have a genuine choice?

04 / 16 · 12–15 min · Understand

Pre-qualification, pre-approval, and final approval

Ask agents to explain the stages without reading them. Correct the idea that a branded letter is a guarantee. Do not claim that all other lenders use unverified information. Fully underwritten pre-approval is another level of review to discuss with the loan officer. Ask: what would you want to know before relying on any letter?

Ask the room: Partner prompt: Explain the difference in one sentence.

05 / 16 · 15–18 min · Understand

What Zillow Home Loans offers your buyer

Keep the ecosystem explanation to one minute. Ask: which of these benefits matters to Jordan right now? Accept a buyer-specific answer. Remove the implication that software removes all friction or that a particular letter automatically wins an offer. A named loan officer and a clear next action are the practical value.

Ask the room: Ask: Which benefit would Jordan actually care about?

06 / 16 · 18–21 min · Understand

Questions for the loan officer

The source deck’s 585 floor conflicts with the current FAQ, which states 620 for most loan types. Do not replace one blanket cutoff with another in agent scripts. Route eligibility to the lender. The FAQ contains inconsistent wording about hard checks, but explicitly allows circumstances requiring a hard inquiry. Do not use “soft pull always.” Keep product exclusions, exact terms, fee claims and special pricing in the reference review, not memory drills.

Ask the room: Quick check: Who confirms eligibility and the credit inquiry?

07 / 16 · 21–23 min · Understand

How would you explain the credit check?

Pause before clicking. Ask agents to rewrite one of the wrong answers in their own voice. Take two answers. Move on once they can state a benefit without a promise. If most miss it, return to the preceding slide for a 30-second clarification.

Ask the room: No notes: rewrite one of the other sentences.

Participant task: Choose the sentence you would use with a real buyer.

  • “Zillow never needs a hard credit pull.”
  • “Pre-qualification uses a soft check. Let’s ask the loan officer what happens after that.”
  • “Once you have the letter, financing is guaranteed.”

After submission: “Pre-qualification uses a soft check. Let’s ask the loan officer what happens after that.” Correct. Explain the initial step, then let the loan officer confirm what this buyer’s process requires.

08 / 16 · 23–27 min · Practice

Responding to common concerns

Have an agent answer before revealing each coaching example. Ask the buyer role: did that address your actual concern? If not, ask a question instead of pushing harder. Cover all three briefly. On comparison shopping, do not promise to beat a rate or say a comparison is automatically free of credit impact. Have each agent submit one response. Reveal one relevant example, then ask them to retry a specific phrase. Do not measure success by whether the buyer accepts an introduction.

Ask the room: Choose an objection. Let the room answer before revealing.

Participant task: Choose one buyer concern. Respond in a way that helps them decide.

  • Which concern are you responding to, and what would you say?

Reveal after the first response:

Already has a lender: “What do you like about working with them, and is there anything you still want clarity on?” If useful, offer an optional second perspective without promising savings. Credit concern: “Is your concern about the first step, or about what happens later in the application?” Let the loan officer explain the process; do not promise every step is free of credit impact. Required lender: “You choose your lender. My role is to help you understand your options. An introduction is available if you want it.” If they decline, respect it and discuss the next step with their chosen lender.

09 / 16 · 27–30 min · Practice

Make the introduction and confirm the call

Ask: if we send this and hear nothing, who owns the next move? The agent owns checking that the introduction landed, not promising a loan outcome. Do not put financial documents or sensitive account information into a group message. Use the approved lender channel for the application.

Ask the room: Ask: Where do handoffs disappear on our team?

11 / 16 · 38–41 min · Apply

Follow up on incomplete introductions

The original deck specifies 4% conversion, 25% pickup, 10% pre-approval, and a rolling 90-day window. Those exact thresholds, attribution rules, and lead consequences were not established by current public sources. Confirm them in the team’s live dashboard with the program owner. Explain numerator and denominator using the example, without presenting it as a policy. Do not imply agents control approval decisions or should change statuses to improve a metric. Show the illustrative calculation briefly. Spend the discussion on stalled introductions, missing consent or owners, and the next useful action rather than testing arithmetic.

Ask the room: Ask: Which stalled introduction can you move forward?

12 / 16 · 41–44 min · Apply

Check financing before writing an offer

Give 30 seconds to answer before revealing. Invite one person to act as Jordan and another as the agent. Ask Jordan to repeat what happens next; if they cannot, the agent should clarify the handoff. Reinforce that a lender’s average speed is not a promise for this transaction.

Ask the room: Ask the buyer role to repeat the next step back.

Participant task: Return to Jordan. The buyer wants to write tonight.

  • Your two-sentence update

Reveal after the first response:

“The loan officer is checking the details for this property and what is still needed. Let’s confirm those items and a workable timeline before we rely on financing terms in the offer.”

13 / 16 · 44–47 min · Retain

What do you remember?

Give 60 seconds with no help. Have partners compare from memory for 30 seconds. Reveal and ask them to fill a gap in a different color on paper, or revise their text. This revisits key ideas after a delay for retrieval, not another lecture. If a gap is common, ask for a fresh example instead of rereading.

Ask the room: Recall first. Reveal after everyone has tried.

Participant task: Write first. Compare second. Fill the gaps.

  • 1 · What are the four conversation steps?
  • 2 · What does a pre-approval not guarantee?
  • 3 · What makes a handoff complete?

Reveal after the first response:

Reflect → Explain → Ask → Confirm. A pre-approval is conditional; it does not guarantee funding. A complete handoff includes permission, an identified loan officer, a confirmed next step and owner, and a follow-up time.

14 / 16 · 47–50 min · Retain

How would you answer Jordan?

Vote before selecting. Ask someone who chose correctly to explain why the other answers fall short. Finish with a 30-second individual spoken response. The multiple-choice answer is only a check; the spoken response demonstrates the skill. Use the four-behavior rubric if an agent needs another attempt.

Ask the room: Ask: Why would Jordan be concerned about this?

Participant task: Show that you can apply the lesson.

  • “Yes, it’s part of our program.”
  • “No, but they will definitely save you money.”
  • “You choose your lender. If a second conversation would help, may I introduce you to a loan officer at Zillow Home Loans?”

After submission: “You choose your lender. If a second conversation would help, may I introduce you to a loan officer at Zillow Home Loans?” Yes. You respected the decision and offered a concrete, optional next step. Now confirm the contact method and time only if Jordan agrees.

What to do after this training

Close on the behavior, not the team metric. Ask for one final takeaway in the buyer’s language. Keep the resource sheet available for questions. If an agent did not demonstrate all four behaviors, schedule another brief practice before marking the skill ready in your own coaching process. This workshop does not issue a certification.
