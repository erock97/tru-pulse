Day 3 · A 75-minute live workshop

Help the buyer understand what works, what does not, and what to do next.

From a first meeting to a useful plan

We will learn the approach, discuss the difficult moments, then practice the full conversation.

Before the visit

Prepare useful comparisons and set expectations.

At the home

Give the buyer room to react, then understand those reactions.

Before you leave

Connect priorities, concerns, and timing to an agreed plan.

Practice together

Take turns as agent, buyer, and observer. Give feedback and retry.

Give each home a reason to be on the tour

Use what the buyer told you to propose two or three useful comparisons.

Connect to a need

“You wanted room to work from home. This one has an enclosed office.”

Name the tradeoff

“The yard is smaller than you asked for. Would comparing the layout still be useful?”

Confirm the visit

Check access, showing instructions, the meeting plan, and the applicable brokerage-approved agreement.

Make it safe to dislike the home

“Drop the rope” means the buyer can be honest without having to defend their reaction.

“I’m excited to show you around. You do not have to make this home work. Tell me what you like and what you would change. That helps us figure out what to look for next.”

Let them experience the home

Stay available, notice their reactions, and contribute information that helps them decide.

Give them room

Let the buyer look and talk. You do not need to narrate every room.

Follow their reaction

“You paused in the office. What were you picturing using it for?”

Separate fact from guess

If a property question is unresolved, say so and explain how you will seek the answer.

Questions that make the next home a better fit

Choose a question that follows the buyer’s answer. You do not need to ask the whole list.

Understand the use

“How would you use this space?” · “What is difficult about your current setup?”

Find the difference

“What worked better in the other home?” · “What made this one feel different in person?”

Explore priorities

“Which part would you miss most?” · “What would make that tradeoff worth considering?”

Check your understanding

“So a separate workspace matters more than an extra bedroom—is that right?”

Ask for a few minutes before they leave the house

Make the purpose clear: use today’s reactions to decide what would help next.

“Thanks for being honest about the house. Do you have a couple of minutes? I’d like to understand what worked and what didn’t so we can make the next step useful for you.”

Ask what did not work about the home

Fictional example: the buyer rejects the first home. The agent uses that answer to learn what a better fit would look like.

Buyer

“The kitchen felt dated, and the yard was too small.”

Agent

“What do you need the yard to let you do?”

Buyer

“We want room for our two dogs to run. We do not want a renovation project.”

Agent

“If the yard worked and the kitchen was ready to use, how close would that get us? What else would still be missing?”

Reasons buyers may hesitate after a showing

These statements begin a conversation. Learn what is behind them before deciding how to help.

  • “We would rather keep renting.”
  • “We are about six months out.”
  • “I am unsure about interest rates.”
  • “I do not know what payment we can afford.”
  • “We may need to relocate for work.”
  • “We need to sell our current home first.”

Understand the concern before proposing a solution

Work with the buyer toward a useful plan at their pace.

Acknowledge the concern

Recognize what matters to them: “I understand wanting to feel prepared before taking that step.”

Ask questions to understand

“What is driving that timing?” · “What would you need to know?” · “How important is moving before your lease ends?”

Offer help and agree on a plan

Connect help to their answer. That may mean lender input, research, a home-sale plan, or preparing over the next six months.

Connect the next step to the buyer’s goal

Fictional example: the buyer wants to move before their lease ends in six months, but is unsure what interest rates would mean for their payment.

“You said moving before your lease ends matters to you. Would it help to talk with a lender about the payment and options? We may learn something neither of us knew to consider. If you would like an introduction, I can arrange it, and we can reconnect afterward to decide what makes sense for you.”

Can the buyer explain the plan back to you?

A useful next step answers these questions in plain English.

Why this step?

What did we learn today, and what question or need does this address?

Who does what?

What will you do? What has the buyer agreed to do? What remains unresolved?

When do we reconnect?

Agree on a time or a clear trigger and a useful way to communicate.

Save what changed and what you promised

The next contact should have a valid business reason connected to this buyer.

Factual note

Record what worked, what did not, the reason for timing, and the agreed plan. Keep unknowns unknown.

Follow-through

Use a dated FUB task to support the agreed action. Deliver the update even if an outside answer is still pending.

Buyer practice cards

Case 1 · The home does not fit

“We liked the living area, but I could hear everything from the office. We are still figuring out what matters.”

Buyer facts: one buyer takes work calls at home; a quiet enclosed office matters more than an extra bedroom. They want to compare two quieter layouts next Saturday if access works. Reveal the reason when asked. Agent: learn what “quiet” requires and agree on a search and confirmation plan; do not promise access.

Case 2 · The timing has a reason

“We like the home, but we keep saying a year. I do not know what payment we would be comfortable with.”

Buyer facts: a year is an estimate, not a fixed deadline. They have not spoken to a lender and want information before deciding whether buying fits. They are open to an introduction, not committed to buying. Agent: clarify the concern, offer relevant preparation, and agree how to reconnect without accelerating their decision.

Case 3 · An answer is holding up the decision

“This one could work, but the roof worries me. I need to understand that before deciding whether to take the next step.”

Buyer facts: the roof age is unknown. They would like to consider an offer soon if they can understand the issue, but have not decided to offer. They can talk again at six. Agent: establish what information matters and how urgent it is, seek appropriate help, and agree on an update without promising the outside answer.

What each signal means

Connection status is separate from participation. A submitted answer is separate from a completed action. A record check is separate from writing quality. Record who observed a speaking turn and who reviewed it; a peer observation or self-check is not coach sign-off. Practice readiness, quiz certification, and activation remain separate.
