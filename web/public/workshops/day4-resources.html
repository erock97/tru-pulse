What Zillow Home Loans does

Zillow Home Loans is Zillow’s affiliated mortgage lender.

For the buyer

A loan officer reviews financing options, explains the payment and cash needed, and guides the loan process. ZHL offers purchase and refinance loans.

For the agent

You coordinate property questions, financing progress and transaction deadlines with the loan officer. The loan officer determines loan eligibility and approval.

For this training

Learn what ZHL provides so you can explain the reason for an introduction. The buyer decides which lender to use.

Know the loan options you can ask ZHL about

Name the option, then let the loan officer explain whether it fits this buyer.

Conventional

A mortgage without a federal government guarantee. Ask about available fixed-rate and adjustable-rate options.

FHA

Insured by the Federal Housing Administration. Ask about down payment and mortgage insurance when comparing it with a conventional loan.

VA

For eligible veterans, service members and certain surviving spouses. The loan officer confirms eligibility and explains any funding fee.

Jumbo

A loan above the applicable conforming limit. Ask which limit applies to the property and what ZHL requires for the larger amount.

ZHL also offers refinance options. Rates, down payments and program requirements must be confirmed for the individual file. ZHL currently lists availability in U.S. states except New York; territories are excluded.

Understand the stages before calling a buyer approved

Ask what the lender has reviewed and what still needs to happen.

01

Pre-qualification

An initial estimate using self-reported income and assets plus a soft credit check. It does not mean the documents have all been verified.

02

Verified pre-approval

The lender verifies income, assets and credit. ZHL describes a soft-pull option; confirm the inquiry type and remaining conditions with the loan officer.

03

Final approval and funding

The lender must complete the remaining borrower and property requirements. A pre-approval letter does not guarantee funding.

ZHL pre-qualification and pre-approval FAQ ↗ · Reviewed September 17, 2026

How ZHL reviews credit during pre-approval

Pre-approval includes verification of income, assets and credit.

Soft-pull pre-approval

ZHL describes a pre-approval option that uses a soft credit inquiry. That inquiry does not affect the buyer’s credit score.

When an earlier hard pull may help

If the buyer is close to the loan program’s minimum credit score, the loan officer may recommend a hard pull before issuing the letter. The purpose is to resolve eligibility concerns before the buyer shops relying on that approval.

What to confirm with the loan officer

Ask: “Does this buyer need a hard pull before you issue the pre-approval? What minimum score applies to their program?” Confirm the answer before describing the credit check to the buyer.

A soft inquiry and a hard inquiry are different. A hard inquiry can affect the credit score. The loan officer determines when it is needed; do not assume it always waits until the buyer is ready to make an offer.

Decide whether a lender introduction fits the timing

TRU’s introduction guidance: confirm the buyer’s plans before transferring the lead.

01

Buying within six months

If the buyer wants to purchase, has a financing question and wants help, offer an introduction. A short timeline alone is not permission to transfer.

02

More than six months away

Usually continue agent follow-up rather than transfer now. At twelve months out, ask what sets the timing, save the reason and agree when to revisit financing.

03

Already supported or not interested

If they have a lender and do not want another conversation, respect that. Confirm what is already in progress and continue working with their chosen lender.

This is TRU’s timing guidance, not a ZHL lending requirement. If a buyer asks for financing advice earlier, clarify the question and offer a conversation when it would help.

Ask about the buyer’s plan when they raise a credit concern

Do not automatically transfer someone because they mention a credit problem.

No near-term purchase or request for help

Ask when they expect to buy and whether they want help now. If they are not ready to act, agree on a follow-up date instead of sending an unexpected lender referral.

Wants to buy soon and asks about options

For “I want to buy in three months and need to know what is possible,” offer a loan-officer conversation about eligibility and steps. Confirm that they want the introduction.

“Are you looking for help understanding your options now, or would you rather revisit this closer to when you’re ready?”

Let the loan officer assess credit and loan eligibility. Do not promise approval, a workaround or a credit-repair timeline.

Offer the introduction at the end of the appointment

First ask: “Have you already spoken with a lender about your budget?”

If they need a lender
“Since you’re hoping to buy in the next three months, it would help to know what payment you’re comfortable with and what you can qualify for. I work with a loan officer at Zillow Home Loans. Would you like me to introduce you?”
If they say yes
“Would you prefer a group text or email? What time would be good for a call?”

Use their answer in the introduction. Ask the loan officer to confirm the time before telling the buyer it is scheduled.

If they decline: “No problem. How would you like to handle financing from here?” The buyer chooses the lender.

Send an introduction that explains why they are connecting

Sample wording for the practice case. Replace the names, goal and time with what the buyer agreed to.

Group text or email

Jordan, meet Casey, a loan officer with Zillow Home Loans.

Casey, Jordan is hoping to buy in the next three months and wants to understand the monthly payment and cash needed. Jordan asked me to connect you.

Jordan is free tomorrow at three. Casey, does that work for a call?

After sending

Confirm that Casey received the introduction and replied about the time. Tell Jordan when the call is confirmed.

If there is no reply

Contact the loan officer directly. Update the buyer at the time you promised and agree on another time if needed.

Save the follow-up

Record the buyer’s permission, the introduction and the call status in FUB. Create a task to check whether the call happened.

Explain ZHL in your own words

The loan officer can explain loan options, payment and cash needed. An estimate is a starting point; verified financing involves supporting documents and conditions. Confirm outstanding requirements and property-specific timing before relying on the letter.

How would you bring up the lender at the first appointment?

Ask whether they already have a lender and what they need to know about financing. Connect the introduction to their near-term purchase goal, identify Zillow Home Loans, ask permission, and confirm a contact method and time. Do not transfer them just because they attended a showing.

Review what happened in the introductions

Buyer practice cards

Jordan · first appointment, buying within three months

I definitely want to keep looking and buy a home. I have not spoken with a lender yet. I want to understand the monthly payment and cash I will need.

Buyer: accept an introduction if the agent explains the reason and asks. Prefer a group text and offer tomorrow at three. Loan officer/observer: confirm that time only when asked. Give one correction and let the agent retry.

Morgan · twelve months away

I am only looking ahead. My job move is about a year away, and I do not need financing help right now.

Agent: ask what sets the timing, respect that no lender help is wanted now, and agree when to revisit. Do not transfer simply to complete a referral.

Alex · credit concern and a three-month goal

I want to buy in the next three months, but I have a credit issue and need to understand whether I have any options.

Agent: ask whether Alex wants a loan officer to review the options. Explain the purpose and get permission; do not promise approval or decide credit eligibility yourself.

Taylor · already has a lender

I already have a lender I trust, and I do not want another introduction.

Agent: respect the decision. Ask what has already been completed and agree how you will coordinate with the chosen lender.
